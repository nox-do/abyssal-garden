# Abyssal Garden — nox-do fork

**A dreamy underwater world you can swim through, right in your browser.**

Towering kelp forests, sunbeams pouring through a rippling surface, a sunken ship, manta rays gliding overhead.
Everything is generated by code: no external images, model files or sound files. The whole world still fits in **one self-contained HTML file: 110,422 bytes (107.834 KiB), or 44,704 bytes with gzip**.

Based on [Abyssal Garden by Maksymilian Dendura (Noniv)](https://github.com/Noniv/abyssal-garden). This is [nox-do’s modified fork](https://github.com/nox-do/abyssal-garden), with changes to exploration, rendering, wildlife and memory use. The original author’s MIT copyright and support link are preserved.

## Try it

**[Play our version](https://nox-do.github.io/abyssal-garden/)** — desktop Chrome or Edge with WebGPU.

For offline use:

1. Download [`index.html`](index.html).
2. Open it in **Chrome** or **Edge** on a desktop computer.
3. Wait a few seconds while the world is built, then press **Dive in** and start swimming.

Nothing to install, and it works offline. It needs a browser with **WebGPU**. If you see *"This scene needs WebGPU"*, update your browser or switch to Chrome / Edge.

## What's down there

- **Kelp forests** that sway in the current and sparkle with tiny gold and green specks
- **Light rays** streaming down from a wavy surface, with shimmering caustics on the sea floor
- **A sunken wooden ship**, a **giant stone whale tail**, rock arches, canyons and drifting jellyfish
- **Manta rays**, schools of fish, rising bubbles and drifting particles
- **A purple grotto** with glowing magenta ground cover, coral and barrel sponges
- **A soundscape** made up as you listen: a deep rumble, distant whale calls, soft chimes and bubbles

## Controls

| | Keyboard & mouse |
|---|---|
| Look around | Move the mouse |
| Swim | `W` `A` `S` `D` |
| Up / down | `E` / `Q` |
| Swim faster | Hold `Shift` |
| Jump to a viewpoint | `1` to `6` (the spots in the screenshots) |
| Menu | `Esc` releases the mouse and opens the menu with the list of controls; **Resume** takes you back |
| Sound on / off | `M` (sound starts when you dive in) |
| Performance stats | `H` |
| Detail level | **Low**, **Medium** or **High** in the menu (reloads the page) |

The seabed, cliffs and arches are solid: you glide along the rock instead of swimming into it, and you still fit through every tunnel and arch. Kelp, plants, corals, sponges, loose rocks, jellyfish, the ship and the whale tail you can swim through.

**On a touchscreen:** hold one finger down to swim forward and slide it to steer. Put a second finger down to go faster. The `≡` button in the corner opens the menu. Phones always show the scene in landscape and start on Low or Medium detail. (Touch support is experimental.)

## Detail levels

The **Low**, **Medium** and **High** buttons in the menu pick the detail level; the current one is lit. Each level is the same world, built with less in it:

| Level | What it is | Picked on a first visit for |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | The full scene | Computers |
| **Medium** | Half of the leaves, sparkles and pebbles; small rocks, sponges and corals cast no shadows | Phones with 8 GB of memory |
| **Low** | A quarter of the leaves, no anti-aliasing, coarser rocks and corals, simpler lighting, 480p to start with | Other phones, and computers with 4 GB of memory or less |

If loading crashes the tab, or the graphics driver gives up, the page starts again one level lower. Your browser remembers the level, and the resolution and draw distance the scene settled on, for the next visit.

## How it works

- The seabed is sculpted on your graphics card every time the page opens, then decorated with about two million kelp leaves and plants, tens of thousands of rocks, and thousands of sponges, corals and fish.
- Everything is drawn with **WebGPU** directly. No game engine or 3D library is used.
- The ship, whale tail, mantas, fish and corals are built from simple mathematical shapes.
- The sound is synthesized live with the Web Audio API.
- The original adaptive renderer aims for 60 fps by lowering resolution and then draw distance in small steps. It tunes the scene behind the start menu. Performance depends on your browser, GPU, detail level and view; the fork’s measurements are described below.

## What this fork changes

- **Surface and explore the coast.** Swim up with `E` to see the sky, sun and water from above; dive again with `Q`. A visible rocky coastline and one western sand cove extend the underwater setting. Eye height stops at +0.28 m: this is swimming, with shore collision and no walking on land. Surface reflections use the sky; coastal reflections and full refraction are not simulated. [Surface and beach study](docs/surface-study.md).
- **Calmer, more readable organic materials.** Reduced detail-normal noise and brightness variation make barrel sponges and corals look less like crumpled rock, using the same geometry and textures. [Material study](docs/visual-quality-study.md).
- **Rounder kelp silhouettes without more triangles.** An analytical contour trims the existing leaf bands. The original bending, waves and current parameters stay intact; deeply twisted leaves can still show internal folds. [Leaf study](docs/leaf-quality-study.md).
- **More life in shallow water.** Seven groups add 56 blue/yellow fish to the existing 4,240 fish, with a small rounded model and outward-facing body normals. Existing schools and movement parameters are retained. [Fish placement](docs/fish-study.md) and [shape study](docs/fish-shape-study.md).
- **Pulsing jellyfish instead of floating boulders.** 33 translucent jellyfish replace 852 deliberately floating rocks; ground rocks stay unchanged. Bell pulses and trailing tentacles animate in the shader, with no per-frame CPU simulation or instance uploads. This is an intentional content change. [Jellyfish study](docs/jellyfish-study.md).
- **Less redundant data.** CPU upload arrays are released after use, object indices use 16 bits, near and far leaves share one GPU buffer, and the static shadow map uses 16-bit depth. Plant density, draw distance and leaf-motion parameters were not reduced to achieve these savings. [Upload/index study](docs/surface-study.md) and [GPU memory study](docs/demoscene-study.md).

## Footprint and GPU measurements

The release is larger than the original because it adds exploration and wildlife. Exact shipping HTML/gzip sizes, hashes and checks are recorded in [the publication report](docs/publication-report.json). Gzip size describes conventional compression, not an embedded loader.

The studies below compare **individual steps with their own immediate baselines**, rather than one cumulative original-versus-release benchmark:

| Integrated change | Recorded result | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Release CPU upload arrays | 2.50–2.71 MiB of array payload released after upload | [Memory results](experiments/surface/results/memory-summary.json) |
| 16-bit object indices | GPU index buffers smaller by 0.45–0.52 MiB; terrain remains 32-bit | [Memory results](experiments/surface/results/memory-summary.json) |
| Shared near/far leaves + 16-bit shadows | Nominal GPU resources −55.005 MiB High, −20.147 MiB Medium, −14.719 MiB Low at 1280×720 | [Demoscene results](experiments/demoscene/results/summary.json) |
| Same memory step, High | 286.581 → 231.576 MiB nominal buffers/textures (−19.2%); shared leaves pixel-identical in 162 comparisons; shadow edges differ slightly | [Method and image checks](docs/demoscene-study.md) |
| Rounded shallow fish, including placement | +1,694 HTML bytes and +4,812 nominal GPU buffer bytes | [Fish shape study](docs/fish-shape-study.md) |
| Jellyfish replacing floating stones | +1,902 HTML bytes, −29,060 GPU buffer bytes; one additional draw per frame | [Jellyfish results](experiments/jellyfish/results/summary.json) |

**GPU render time, not just memory:** the shared-leaf/shadow combination changed sampled GPU medians by −0.131 to +0.131 ms across four views and three levels. Leaf contours cost +0.066 to +0.721 ms in the tested High views. The sand cove cost +0.295 ms in the High close view. Jellyfish reduced High pose 1 from 11.272 to 10.879 ms, while their close view increased from 7.537 to 7.864 ms. Fewer triangles do not automatically mean less fragment work. See the [demoscene](docs/demoscene-study.md), [leaf](docs/leaf-quality-study.md), [beach](docs/surface-study.md) and [jellyfish](docs/jellyfish-study.md) timing tables and their raw JSON reports.

These are local Chrome 157 / Apple Metal measurements on a Mac, generally at 1280×720 with fixed cameras, animation times and detail factor. GPU timestamps measure serialized render passes, **not gameplay FPS**. Array payloads and nominal GPU descriptors are **not process RAM, peak memory or actual driver allocation**. There is no demonstrated general speedup or cold-start improvement. Jellyfish timing required recovery after browser timeouts; its report retains completed cases and documents that limitation. The visual studies separately check all three detail levels, world data, offline startup, menu, movement and audio; test pages suppress pointer lock.

**Experiments are not all shipping features.** The historical 99,514 → 56,506-byte embedded-gzip result belongs to a separate experiment with byte-identical decompressed JavaScript. Neither that loader nor the nearFly alternatives, packed leaf parameters, plant recipes or WGSL-name minification are integrated into this release. [Optimization handoff and history](docs/optimization-handoff.md).

## Make it your own

The readable source code is in the [`source`](source) folder. To change something and rebuild `index.html` you need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 18 or newer:

```bash
cd source
npm ci
npm run build
```

This writes a new `index.html` next to this README. `npm run build:readable` writes an unminified version instead, which is easier to debug in the browser's developer tools.

`npm run build:dev` writes a separate `dev.html` with two extra tools that are not part of `index.html`: `F` flies the camera along a one-minute path through the whole world, and `B` runs that flight as a benchmark and reports frame and GPU times.

| File | What's inside |
|---|---|
| [`source/src/world.js`](source/src/world.js) | The world layout: rock walls, arches, tunnels, kelp forests, sponge gardens, fish schools, mantas, the ship and the whale tail. **A good place to start.** |
| [`source/src/shaders.js`](source/src/shaders.js) | The look: water and fog colors, lighting, kelp, rocks, the water surface and light rays (WGSL shader code) |
| [`source/src/main.js`](source/src/main.js) | The renderer, the camera, the controls, the six viewpoints and the camera path the world is laid out around |
| [`source/src/audio.js`](source/src/audio.js) | The soundscape |
| [`source/src/quality.js`](source/src/quality.js) | The detail levels: which one a device starts on, and what is remembered between visits |
| [`source/src/math.js`](source/src/math.js) | Noise, random numbers and mesh-building helpers |
| [`source/src/dev.js`](source/src/dev.js) | Development tools (fly-through and benchmark), only in `dev.html` |
| [`source/template.html`](source/template.html) | The page around the scene: loading bar, start menu, links and their styles |

Some easy first tweaks:

- **Water color:** `WCOL` (blue-green water) and `PCOL` (purple grotto) near the top of the shared scene code in `shaders.js`
- **Sunlight color:** `SUNC` right next to them
- **Brightness and light rays:** `EXPO` and `RAYS` in `main.js`
- **Swimming speed:** search for `26 : 7` in `main.js` (fast speed : normal speed)
